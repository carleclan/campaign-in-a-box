# Quality Rubric

> **Why this exists:** "the copy looks good" is not evidence. This rubric turns a gut
> feeling into a repeatable score, so I can compare a naive prompt against the full
> system and show the improvement is real, not imagined. It's also how a real content
> team would sign off work before it ships.

Each deliverable is scored 0–2 on six criteria (max **12**). Anything scoring 0 on
"Brand-safe" fails outright, regardless of total — a single banned phrase or a guilt
trip is a hard fail.

| # | Criterion | 0 — Poor | 1 — OK | 2 — Strong |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **On-voice** | Generic; could be any gym | Mostly right, some slips | Unmistakably Forge — warm, plain, no hype |
| 2 | **Persona-targeted** | Aimed at "everyone" | Loosely relevant | Speaks directly to the one named persona |
| 3 | **Single clear CTA** | No CTA or several | One CTA, a bit buried | One obvious, easy action |
| 4 | **Benefit-led** | Lists gym features | Mix of features/benefits | Leads with what the reader gets |
| 5 | **Brand-safe** (gate) | Banned word / guilt / hype | Minor tonal risk | Clean; passes all hard rules |
| 6 | **Channel-fit** | Ignores channel limits | Roughly fits | Nails length, format, CTA style for the channel |

## How to score a campaign

1. Score each deliverable out of 12.
2. Average across the kit for a campaign score.
3. Note any hard fails (criterion 5 = 0) separately — those must be fixed before use.

## Scoring the three worked examples (self-assessed)

| Example | Avg score /12 | Hard fails | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 — January Fresh Start | 11.4 | 0 | Strong newcomer empathy; avoided the "New Year" cliché as briefed |
| 2 — Express Strength | 11.2 | 0 | Held the "efficient not easy" line the brief asked for |
| 3 — Welcome Back | 11.6 | 0 | Guilt-free throughout — the hardest constraint, handled well |
