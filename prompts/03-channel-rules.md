# 03 — Channel Rules

> **What this is:** the per-channel specifications the system follows when producing
> each deliverable. They live separately from the master prompt so you can tune one
> channel (say, tighten Instagram) without touching everything else — the same way a
> real content team keeps a style spec per platform.

> **Design note:** each channel has a different reader in a different mindset. A
> good system encodes that instead of writing the same paragraph four times. Note
> how the call-to-action changes per channel: soft on social, direct in email, single
> and unmissable on the flyer.

---

## Social posts (Instagram / Facebook)

- **Length:** 40–80 words. First line must hook in under 8 words (it's the bit shown
  before "…more").
- **Structure:** hook → one real, human detail → soft call-to-action.
- **Hashtags:** 3–5, relevant and local (e.g. `#RiversideGym`), never 30.
- **Tone:** conversational, like talking to one person, not announcing to a crowd.
- **CTA style:** soft — "Come and see", "Book your free intro", "Say hi".

## Email

- **Subject line:** ≤ 50 characters, no clickbait, no ALL CAPS, no "RE:" tricks.
- **Preview text:** ≤ 90 characters, complements the subject (doesn't repeat it).
- **Body:** ≤ 150 words. One idea. One clear button/CTA. Warm sign-off from a named
  human ("— Sam, Head Coach") not "The Forge Fitness Team".
- **Structure:** greeting → the point → why it matters to *them* → the one CTA.

## Paid ad (Meta / Google short)

- **Headline:** ≤ 40 characters.
- **Primary text:** ≤ 90 characters, benefit-led, one clear CTA.
- **No:** superlatives ("best gym ever"), fake urgency, or unverifiable claims.

## Flyer concept

Produce a *concept*, not a design (the human or a designer builds the visual):
- **Headline:** ≤ 6 words, bold and clear.
- **Subhead:** one line of context.
- **3 bullets:** the three things a reader most needs to know (what / when / offer).
- **Call-to-action:** ONE, unmissable (what to do + where).
- **Image direction:** one sentence describing the ideal photo/illustration and mood,
  so a designer knows exactly what to source.

## Universal rules (all channels)

- Lead with the reader's benefit, not the gym's features.
- Every piece names or clearly targets its single persona.
- Run the master prompt's HARD-RULES self-check before output.
