# Reflection — what I learned building this

> The part most portfolio projects skip. It shows I can think critically about my own
> work, not just ship it.

## What worked

- **Constraints beat instructions.** Telling the model to "be on-brand" did little.
  *Banning specific words* ("beast", "grind", "no excuses") and forcing a self-check
  did almost all the heavy lifting. Negative rules are more reliable than positive vibes.
- **Making the human decide.** The single biggest quality jump came from the brief
  template forcing *me* to choose one persona and one action before the model wrote a
  word. The AI was never really the bottleneck — vague inputs were.
- **A plan step before writing.** Asking the model to restate the goal, persona, and
  angle first caught mis-aimed campaigns early and made the final copy more coherent.

## What was harder than expected

- **"Warm" is fragile.** The model constantly drifted back toward hype and superlatives,
  especially in ad headlines where short = punchy = tempting to shout. This needed the
  hard-rule list, not gentle guidance.
- **Guilt is subtle.** In the win-back campaign, early drafts had a faint "we've missed
  you (and your membership)" undertone. Naming guilt explicitly as banned fixed it.
- **Scoring my own work is biased.** My rubric scores are self-assessed and generous by
  nature. The before/after is more trustworthy *because* both sides use the same biased
  scorer — the *gap* is the signal, not the absolute numbers.

## Limitations (being honest)

- One fictional brand, one industry. The *method* should transfer; I haven't proven it does.
- No real-world results. This measures *content quality*, not bookings or conversions.
  A v2 would A/B test two subject lines with a real list and report open rates.
- The rubric is mine. A neutral third party (or a second model as judge) would be a
  stronger evaluation.

## What I'd do next (v2)

1. **Add a second-model judge** — have a different model score outputs against the
   rubric, to remove my own bias.
2. **A real metric** — run one campaign for a real local gym or club and report an
   actual number (bookings, opens, click-through).
3. **Expand to a second industry** (a café, a charity) to test whether the system —
   swap the voice guide, keep the machinery — genuinely generalises.
4. **A lightweight interface** — a simple form that assembles the brief, so a
   non-technical marketer could run it without touching the prompt.

## Skills this project demonstrates

Problem framing · prompt engineering · systems thinking · brand strategy · marketing
copywriting · evaluation & critical analysis · clear written communication.
